How it works

A school fundraiser in 3 steps

Keep it simple: one live event, one link to share, everyone tuning in from wherever they are.

01

Get the OK, then sign up

Check with your advisor or student activities office first. Then a group officer who is 18 or older signs up through the link below, installs the app, and claims an @username for the club.

02

Host a live room

Plan a trivia night, a talent show, a Q&A with a coach or alum, or a study jam. Open it as a live audio or video room in the app's live-room feature (which it calls “Call”). You can set an entry fee or leave it free and accept tips.

03

Share it & track it

Post your club's @username and room to supporters. Entry fees and tips go to the group's wallet in PX1. Report the totals to your treasurer and advisor, and remember PX1's value isn't guaranteed (see below).

Why student groups like it

Built for live, social fundraising

These are the features powerx.one lists today. Some parts of the app are still in beta or on its roadmap.

🎤

Events with no venue

Live audio and video rooms let alumni, families, and friends join from anywhere. No room booking needed.

🎟️

Built-in ticketing

powerx.one describes “pay-to-enter rooms” that gate access with a single action, so you don't have to stitch together a payment tool and a call tool.

💸

Tips in the moment

Supporters can tip during the event, which suits shout-outs, challenges, and “unlock the next round” moments.

🔑

Your group holds the keys

The wallet is self-custodial, so powerx.one doesn't hold the funds. That also means your group must protect its recovery phrase. Lose it and the funds are gone.

Know before you go

The honest fine print

Read this before your group signs up.

⚠️ Crypto rewards carry risk

Rooms and tips are priced in PX1, powerx.one's own token. As of October 2026, PX1 had not launched or been listed on any exchange. powerx.one says it makes no promise the token will have any value. Money raised this way is not guaranteed to be worth anything in dollars.

🚫 Don't buy tokens with club money

The sign-up link opens powerx.one's dashboard, which also sells PX1 in a “Public Round.” Running a fundraiser doesn't require buying anything. Token purchases are final and non-refundable. Never use group or school funds to buy PX1.

🏫 Check school policy first

Many schools need approval for fundraisers, have rules about how club money is handled, or don't allow crypto. Get written OK from your advisor or student activities office before you launch.

FAQ

Questions, answered

What exactly is powerx.one?

powerx.one calls itself “the self-custodial wallet where your community pays you.” It's a mobile app that combines a multi-chain crypto wallet with live audio and video rooms (which it calls “Call”), tipping, and chat, with a social feed and music on its roadmap. Its own token is called PX1. Android is on Google Play and iOS is a TestFlight beta.

Who can sign up?

Only people 18 or older. powerx.one's Terms of Service require users to be at least 18 and to make sure their use is legal where they live. Groups with members under 18 should not use it, so this isn't a fit for most high school clubs.

Does it cost anything?

The app is free to download. The dashboard the link opens also sells the PX1 token. Buying tokens is optional and isn't needed to host rooms or receive tips. If you or your group ever do buy, powerx.one's terms say purchases are final and non-refundable, and blockchain network fees may apply to some transactions.

Can we turn what we raise into cash for our club?

Not reliably right now. Tips and entry fees are in PX1, and as of October 2026 PX1 hadn't launched or been listed on an exchange. powerx.one shows a “listing target” but says it's a target, not a guarantee. Treat any PX1 you receive as having uncertain value, and don't promise donors a dollar amount.

Who should control the club's wallet?

Because the wallet is self-custodial, whoever has the recovery phrase controls the funds, and powerx.one can't recover a lost phrase. Agree with your advisor and treasurer on who holds it, store it securely, and never share it with anyone. powerx.one says it will never ask for it.

Do we need school approval?

Probably. Most colleges and high schools have rules for fundraising, handling club money, and using outside payment platforms, and some don't allow crypto. Check with your student activities office or club advisor before you start.
